Our purpose

Government should be easier to navigate.

Knowing where to turn shouldn't require knowing how every level of government works.

Why Civic Compass exists

A broken heater, a stalled benefits claim, a school that stopped providing services: these problems usually have someone in government whose job is to help. Finding that someone is the hard part. Responsibility is divided among city departments, state agencies, federal programs and elected officials, and the right one depends on both the issue and where you live. A single ZIP code can even fall in several legislative districts at once. For many people, this puzzle is a real barrier to taking part in civic life: they write to the wrong office, wait for a reply that never comes, or give up.

What it helps you do

Civic Compass is a tool for making civic engagement more accessible. It helps people:

  • Find the officeIdentify the appropriate government office or agency for a specific concern.
  • Verify the contactFind contact information drawn from official sources, with the source and date checked.
  • Understand whySee a plain-language explanation of why that office is relevant.
  • Write clearlyPrepare clear, personalized correspondence based on the facts they provide.

Your voice, your decision

Civic Compass supports civic participation; it does not replace the person taking part. It drafts, and you decide. Every letter can be reviewed and edited, and nothing is copied or downloaded until you confirm you have checked it. Civic Compass never sends a message or submits a form on anyone's behalf. You stay in control of what you say, to whom, and when.

Our commitments

  • Accessible civic engagementPlain-language questions, no knowledge of government structure required, and a design built for phones and keyboards, with labelled form controls.
  • Nonpartisan assistanceRecommendations are based on which office has responsibility for an issue, not on party or politics. Civic Compass takes no position on policy.
  • Accuracy and verified sourcesOffices and officials come only from official records kept on file. The AI chooses among those records; it never writes contact details itself.
  • TransparencyEvery recommendation shows its reasoning, its source and the date it was checked. When data is limited or a ZIP code is ambiguous, Civic Compass says so instead of guessing.
  • User privacy and controlNames, addresses and descriptions are not stored. A street address is optional and used only to identify exact districts.

Where we are today Today

Civic Compass is a pilot. It covers federal offices and members of Congress nationwide, all Connecticut state legislators and key state agencies, and city departments in New Haven, Connecticut. Outside the pilot area it shows what it can verify and links to official directories.

Where we want to go Future

The long-term goal is to grow from the Connecticut and New Haven pilot to comprehensive nationwide coverage across all 50 states. None of the following is available yet; these are planned directions:

  1. State legislators and government agencies nationwide, verified from official sources for every state.
  2. Municipal departments and local officials across U.S. cities and counties.
  3. More detailed, issue-specific agency routing, so each concern reaches the most precise office.
  4. Regular automatic updates to officeholders, jurisdiction records and official contact information, with change detection.
  5. Broader accessibility and language support, starting with Spanish.

Interactive jurisdiction overlays Future

Today's map shows only the approximate area of a ZIP code. A future version could let people view congressional, state legislative, municipal and other relevant government boundaries directly on the map, to understand the area each office represents and how jurisdictions overlap. These overlays would use authoritative geographic data and would make clear that identifying exact representation may still require a full street address.
